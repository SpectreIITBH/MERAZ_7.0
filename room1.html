INCIDENT LOG // 23:17 IST
"NEXUS is offline. We have no breach, no attacker, and no reason for this message. Find the first person who knew it was coming."
— Commander Ila Sen, Incident Commander
At 23:17, every display connected to the Meraz NEXUS festival platform went dark. When they came back — seven seconds later — the screens showed a message that no administrator had written:
THE SHOW MUST NOT CONTINUE
WE ARE NOT THE FIRST
The festival's digital noticeboard has been replaced. Every page looks official. Every page was modified before anyone on the SPECTRE team knew something was wrong.

Your mission: Inspect the public-facing festival pages, their metadata, and the digital trail they left behind. Someone prepared for this breach months in advance. Find them.

1.3 The False Official SOURCE VALIDATION 25 PTS ▾
MISSION BRIEFING
Two versions of the NEXUS status page exist. One is the real system page maintained by Rhea's engineering team. The other appeared three days ago and has been silently redirecting volunteer queries. They look almost identical. Almost.
TASK
Compare the official NEXUS page with the mirror page. Identify the specific discrepancies that reveal which page is the impersonation: domain inconsistencies, certificate differences, subtle text changes, and a hidden redirect.
EVIDENCE FILES
INTELLIGENCE HINTS
"Official pages and their copies differ in small but critical ways. Compare everything, not just the text."
"Check the page's <meta> tags, link href values, and any embedded scripts. One page has something the other doesn't."
"Look at the domain in the mirror page's <base> tag and SSL certificate comment. The domain ends in .in.net, not .in."
SUBMIT FLAG
◈ INTEL UNLOCKED

The mirror was registered under the domain nexus-meraz.festival.in.net — close enough to look legitimate, different enough to be a separate server entirely. The mirror's SSL certificate was issued by Parallax CA (not a real certificate authority). The page also contains a 1-pixel tracking beacon that reports to an external endpoint. This is the Curator's reconnaissance infrastructure — built to watch who visits before the attack begins.
