Analysis — Why the Message Worked

Signals That Made It Convincing (Attacker's Craft)
Real ticket reference: The message cited #NX-2847 — KABIR's actual support ticket. This required access to the internal ticket system.
Accurate problem description: "Clock drift after 30 minutes" matches KABIR's reported symptoms exactly.
Correct naming convention: "Ethereal Sync Helper" follows the NEXUS utility naming pattern (e.g., Ethereal Config, Ethereal Monitor).
Real deadline: The September 28th tech review was a real event on the festival calendar.
Emotional timing: Sent at 11:42 IST on September 27th — less than 24 hours before the review, when KABIR's stress was highest.
Solution framing: The message offered help, not a request. It positioned itself as the answer KABIR was already looking for.
Authority appearance: Formatted as an official NEXUS support response with PGP signature (albeit self-signed).
Indicators That Should Have Revealed It (Missed Under Pressure)
Domain mismatch: Sender used meraz-festival.internal (hyphen) instead of the real meraz_festival.internal (underscore).
External download domain: The download URL pointed to cdn-nexus.parallax-sys.net — not a NEXUS or Meraz domain.
Self-signed PGP: The message had a PGP signature, but the key was self-signed, not from the NEXUS team's verified keyring.
No package registry entry: "Ethereal Sync Helper" does not exist in the NEXUS package registry or internal tool directory.
Admin execution request: Legitimate NEXUS tools do not require administrator privileges to install.
No prior communication: NEXUS support had not responded to #NX-2847 since Rhea's Sep 25 message. This response appeared without a prior update.
