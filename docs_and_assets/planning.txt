Yes. After looking at the current NIGHTJAR Hub World.html and the broader NIGHTJAR plan, I think the homepage has a strong visual identity already, but it currently behaves more like a cool playable demo/hub than the actual event command center you described.

The key shift I would make is:

Keep the pixel-art hub as the cinematic entry experience, but make the homepage around it a real NIGHTJAR Response Cell dashboard.

The story specifically positions players as a response team investigating a live incident, with ten stages that progressively reconstruct the breach. Your existing HTML already has the hub, ten doors, NPCs, dialogue, timer, and room names, so the foundation is there.

1. What I would change first
Current experience

Right now the flow is roughly:

Boot screen → walk around pixel-art map → find door → enter room

That is fun, but a participant could reasonably ask:

What exactly am I supposed to do?
Which room should I enter?
What have I solved?
What evidence have I collected?
How many points do I have?
How much time is left?
What is the overall story?
Where are my CTF challenges?
What happens after I finish a room?

The existing HUD only tells them rooms visited, allies briefed, and basic movement instructions.

Your project plan, however, calls for the portal to handle story, rooms, challenges, hints, submissions, progression and scoring.

So I'd make the homepage communicate those things without destroying the immersive game aesthetic.

Yes — this is the part that matters much more than just making the homepage look good.

For NIGHTJAR, I would treat the website as an event platform, not merely a frontend. The pixel-art hub is only the player-facing shell. Behind it, we need systems for registration, teams, challenge publishing, flag validation, scoring, unlocking, admin control, hints, logs, and failure recovery.

The clean mental model is:

Player → Team → Event Session → Room → Challenge → Submission → Score/Progress

Everything should revolve around that.

1. First decision: players should compete as teams

I would not make every browser/session behave independently.

Suppose the event has:

200 participants
teams of 4
50 teams
10 rooms

10. Handle flag formatting

People will submit:

API race conditions
